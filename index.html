Haati: Meet the Maker

A freelance collaboration for a sustainable e-commerce platform, where I led research synthesis, information architecture, UX, high fidelity UI, and developer hand off.

Role

Sole Product Designer

Client

Haati, India

Timeline

2024 - 2025

Category

E-commerce, B2B, B2C

The Challenge

Marketplaces list the product and bury the maker.

Artisans in India earn around ₹270 a day, well below minimum wage for skilled workers. Four or five middlemen sit between them and the buyer. Online platforms make it worse by treating artisans as product suppliers, stripping away their story and identity as cultural creators. (Source: Crafts Council of India and Institute for Human Development, 2026 study of five states)

When the maker is invisible, so is everything behind the piece. Haati’s vision is to promote sustainability at every stage, and my goal was to weave that into the brand identity.

What I Did

Designed a dual pathway for the user offering a choice between bulk orders while hiring artisans directly or shopping for products while discovering the maker. The goal was to make Haati a platform where the artisan is the product. I designed visual cues that helped buyers meet the maker first, then the craft. This approach helped improve artisan - customer engagement and drive up bulk orders. I built sustainability into the site’s information architecture, surfacing the materials artisans use and the practices Haati follows.

Design Hypothesis

One idea, four bets. Make the maker visible and the buying effortless.

Trust: Making the artisan the face of the brand, builds customer’s trust in the brand’s story.

Clarity: If the home screen offers two clear outcomes, buyers won’t need a label to know where to go.

Visible sustainability: If buyers can browse by material, sustainability becomes a reason to choose, not a label.

Cognitive load: If the product stays in view while buyers fill in a long form, more of them will finish it.

01/ The home screen: one front door, two paths

People arrive at Haati wanting one of two things: a piece made for them, or a piece they can have now. The home screen routes both without building two interfaces.

First attempt

I sketched five ways to route visitors, including a Customer/Artisan toggle, nav tabs and side-by-side buttons. Each asked a question without context and read like settings, not choices. They would confuse first-time users.

Direction: Two paths led by outcome, not by label.

The Decision

Both pathways get equal visual weight.

The reasoning was that an unequal split signals a main path and a side path. Equal weight tells every visitor they’re in the right place, which builds trust. It also lets the company run one interface instead of two.

Final Execution: The home screen

Customise with an Artisan and Shop Our Collection, each card says what you get, not who you are, and each ends in one clear action.

02/ Anatomy of a listing card: Spotlight the maker

Every piece is signed.

The artisan’s name sits on the photo and a handcrafted icon marks each piece, so buyers always know whose hands made it.

Customisation is visible.

A needle icon and a “Customisable” tag mark products buyers can personalise. A filter narrows the list to those.

Each card answers three questions at a glance: who made it, what is it, and can I make it personalise it?

The core inversion

The maker comes first. The craft follows.

Standard marketplaces hide the maker. Here, below every product page, an “About our Artisan” profile gives the maker’s name, region and years of craft.

03/ Shop our collection: Personalise in seconds

The Artisan Page allows customers to connect directly with skilled artisans and collaborate on meaningful, handcrafted pieces. Visitors can explore the artisan’s story, past work, craft expertise, materials and pricing, creating a transparent and personal shopping experience. On customisable products, buyers can add a name, upload their own symbol or bulk order without leaving the page. Anything the fields can’t cover goes to the artisan on WhatsApp or email.

The Risk

A long page stacks the product above a long customisation form. Buyers scroll past the product while filling it in and lose sight of what they are ordering.

The Fix

Implementing a split-screen layout. By pinning the image gallery on the left, the user maintains visual reference while navigating the customisation form on the right.

Final Execution: Shop our Collection

A multi-step commissioning brief (material, size, weight, budget, requirements, reference images) becomes one clear form with one action: Get a quote.

04/ Sustainability you can browse: shop by material, spot it by icon

Haati’s brief was to make sustainability visible. So I built the information architecture around what each piece is made of. Buyers can shop by material (brass, copper, seashells, silver) as well as by product type, and the structure also shows the practices Haati follows. A hand-drawn icon set covers each product and material category, so a buyer can read the range at a glance and the handmade feel stays intact.

Little Aha! moments

This project taught me that good UI isn't just about making things look clean; it's about managing cognitive load. Realizing that users were getting lost in the long-scrolling form was a massive "Aha!" moment for me. It proved that in e-commerce, protecting the user's visual context (by pinning the product image) is just as important as the checkout flow itself.

What I’d measure next

  • Trust: first-time buyer conversion and sign-ups.
  • Cognitive load: quote completion and drop-off by step, with the pinned gallery tested against long scroll.
  • Five buyers on the customisation form, and a few artisans on how they receive and answer quotes.

Vivid Sydney

A University UX design concept for Vivid Sydney. I built a social companion app for visitors who want to experience the festival but have no one to go with, turning a solo night out into a shared one.

Role

UI/UX Designer

Client

Vivid Sydney

Timeline

Aug - Nov, 2024

Category

D2C, Social companion App

What is Vivid Sydney?

Vivid Sydney is a 23 day annual festival celebrating creativity, innovation, and technology. It transforms the city of Sydney with immersive light installations, music, cultural events, and projections along an 8km stretch from the Opera House to Ultimo.

Intent

Designed a intuitive social platform that helps solo attendees connect with like-minded festival-goers in real-time, without compromising their privacy or security in large crowds. I built the iPad experience, specifically designing the social experience feature from wireframes to final high-fidelity UI.

Research & Key Insights

  • Interactive maps and real-time navigation enhance visitor convenience.
  • Live event updates and itinerary planning keep users informed for a smooth experience.
  • Dubai Expo 2020’s Time Capsule feature creates a sense of nostalgia and continuity.
  • The "interactive passport stamp" from Dubai Expo 2020 adds a fun, engaging element.
  • Community engagement through global discussions, forums, and challenges fosters connection.
  • User generated content drives participation and enhances connection.

The Feature Deep-Dive

I designed a single, unified interactive map and moved the navigation to a bottom bar to ensure cross-device consistency. While the architecture was better, displaying people, events, and meet-up CTAs all at once created severe visual clutter. If multiple meetups happened near each other, users were accidentally clicking the wrong ones. I tackled this by creating a vertical scroll on the left which allows users to choose from broadcast, create Vivid meetup, and access forums, enhancing clarity for intuitive navigation. The selected option will be cantered in the scroll, showcasing its importance and establishing visual hierarchy.

01/ Broadcast

I designed a location-based "Broadcast" system. Users can simply drop a temporary announcement on the map without setting up formal profiles. This allows users to see where spontaneous conversations are already happening and join in with zero pressure.

The broadcast feature also enables users to write messages for those nearby, which shows up on the map. The message is displayed in a bubble above the user’s avatar, creating a fun interaction that users can like and participate in.

02/ Vivid Meetup

Vivid Meetups allow users to select between public or invite-only settings, enhancing user control and privacy, which aligns with the design principle of user autonomy. Vivid Meetup allows users to easily create a meetup spot by selecting a location and scheduling a time, with space for additional notes.
