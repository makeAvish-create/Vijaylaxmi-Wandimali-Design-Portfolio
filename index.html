Haati: Meet the Maker

A freelance collaboration for a sustainable e-commerce platform, where I led research synthesis, information architecture, UX, high-fidelity UI, and developer hando ff.

Role

Sole Product Designer

Client

Haati, India

Timeline

2024 - 2025

Category

E-commerce, B2B, B2C

The Challenge

Marketplaces list the product and bury the maker.

Artisans in India earn around ₹270 a day, well below minimum wage for skilled workers. Four or five middlemen sit between them and the buyer. Online platforms make it worse by treating artisans as product suppliers, stripping away their story and identity as cultural creators. (Source: Crafts Council of India and Institute for Human Development, 2026 study of five states)

When the maker is invisible, so is everything behind the piece. Haati’s vision is to promote sustainability at every stage, and my goal was to weave that into the brand identity.

What I Did

Designed a dual pathway for the user offering a choice between bulk orders while hiring artisans directly or shopping for products while discovering the maker. The goal was to make Haati a platform where the artisan is the product. I designed visual cues that helped buyers meet the maker first, then the craft. This approach helped improve artisan - customer engagement and drive up bulk orders. I built sustainability into the site’s information architecture, surfacing the materials artisans use and the practices Haati follows.

Design Hypothesis

One idea, four bets. Make the maker visible and the buying effortless.

Trust: Making the artisan the face of the brand, builds customer’s trust in the brand’s story.

Clarity: If the home screen offers two clear outcomes, buyers won’t need a label to know where to go.

Visible sustainability: If buyers can browse by material, sustainability becomes a reason to choose, not a label.

Cognitive load: If the product stays in view while buyers fill in a long form, more of them will finish it.
